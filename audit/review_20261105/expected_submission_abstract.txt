A mathematical verifier can return zero because an answer is wrong or because verification did not finish. We study whether that distinction survives an RLVR reward pipeline, using a pinned, opt-in Math-Verify path in verl. Persistent-worker injections connect a known sparse-metadata defect to two position-dependent outcomes: a failure flag disappears, or batch assembly raises. All 13 injection cases reproduce on a second platform under real Ray. Porting an open upstream assembly repair preserves the flag in all nine injected rate-limited failure cases; a further patch is needed to keep flags aligned across validation batches. Both repair observability, not rewards. A fixed-response replay of 93,733 responses records 54 library-event rows and one zero-to-one change under longer limits. The corrected report leaves 18 rows unresolved, giving one to nineteen recoveries; supplied pre-correction records reproduce the recovery but not the correction, so this range is conditional on that report. A 22,176-trial replay of human-labelled extracted answers reproduces every historical score on Linux. Natural heavy inputs caused a shared-pool batch abort under relaxed diagnostic limits on the original host, but not in a Linux run with substitute controls. On 72 complete human-labelled response groups with synthetic failures, zero-filling gives a failed correct answer a negative advantage whenever a correct sibling survives; loss-only masking leaves the failed zero in its siblings' baseline; excluding failed rows from group statistics avoids that distortion, and when failures are independent of correctness it leaves more groups without signal. The results argue for testing failure information at the wrapper, assembly and consumer boundaries. They estimate neither training-time incidence nor a learning effect, and longer verification limits are not ground truth.
